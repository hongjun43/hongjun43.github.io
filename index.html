I’m Hong Jun Lee, a first‑year PhD student in Computer Science at King’s College London.

I work on multi-agent reinforcement learning and large language model agents: how agents learn to learn together, what update rules they should follow, how they adapt to each other, and how they reason about one another.

About

I started my PhD in June 2026, supervised by Stefanos Leonardos and Yali Du in the Distributed Artificial Intelligence group at King’s. Before the PhD, I spent two years working as an actuary and a data scientist in London.

My research asks how learning rules should change when other agents are also learning: meta-gradients, self-play and co-evolution, and language-model agents.

I speak English, Mandarin and Malay. To a lesser extent: Japanese, Cantonese and Hokkien. Away from the desk: chess, video games, reading.

Research interests

What I’m interested in and am working on.

01

Learned multi-agent update rules

Meta-gradient learning in a multi-agent setting, where every agent is discovering update rules in the same environment, rather than fixing them by hand.

02

Strategic adaptation to other learners

Agents that treat co-players as learners rather than part of the environment, and adapt to, anticipate, and shape how they learn.

03

Self-play/Co-evolve

Curricula that grow with the solver: opponents and tasks that improve as the agent does, such that self-play keeps producing harder problems and improvement compounds.

04

Strategic LLM agents that reason about other agents

Language-model agents that build models of the agents they interact with, improving strategic reasoning while keeping their behaviour safe and aligned.
